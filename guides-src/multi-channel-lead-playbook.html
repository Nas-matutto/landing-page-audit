01 · The problem
Why lead generation stalls

Most lead lists fail for one of three reasons.

Most businesses find leads in one of two ways: they wait for buyers to find them, or they send a batch of cold emails and hope. Both leave money on the table, and almost always for the same three reasons.

Reason 01

Wrong people

Lists built from a job title alone. They fit on paper but have no reason to care about you today.

Reason 02

Wrong time

A good fit on a bad week. Without a timing signal you are guessing, and most guesses land when nobody is buying.

Reason 03

One touch

A single email in a crowded inbox. Many people who would have said yes never saw it, or saw it on the wrong day.

The fix, in one line

Find people who fit and have a reason to talk now, then show up in more than one place until the timing is right.

02 · Where buyers ask
Source A · Warm leads

Every day, your buyers post the exact question you answer.

People ask their peers before they ask a salesperson. They post on LinkedIn, in subreddits, in Facebook groups and under YouTube videos. Find those posts on the day they go up and you are talking to someone who is already shopping.

Rule 01

Speed beats polish

A good answer in the first few hours gets seen. A perfect answer three days later is buried under twenty others.

Rule 02

Help first, sell later

Answer the question they asked. If your answer is good, they will look at who wrote it.

Rule 03

Follow the house rules

Every group and subreddit has its own. One spammy reply can get you banned from the place your buyers gather.

Find your communities in 15 minutes

Ask your customers

Ask your three best customers where they go for advice. Their answers are your shortlist.

Follow the questions

Search your category plus "reddit" or "group". Note where the same questions keep coming up.

Keep the active ones

Keep places with new questions every week and real answers. Drop the ones full of promotion.

Write down your ideal customer in two sentences

Everything in this playbook gets easier once this is clear. Brief it the way you would brief a new hire:

  • The kind of company, its size and where it is
  • The person you want to reach, by role
  • The problem they have that you solve
  • The signals that say "now"

Example: "Marketing agencies in the UK with 10 to 50 people. I want to reach the founder or MD, especially when they're hiring account managers or launching a new service."

05 · Why multi-channel
The core idea

One message is a lottery ticket. A multi-channel campaign is a plan.

A buyer who ignores one email is not saying no. Usually they are busy, they missed it, or the timing was off. Showing up a few times, in a few places, with something useful each time, is one of the most reliable ways to raise the odds that a good lead turns into a conversation.

Reason 01

More chances to be seen

Inboxes and feeds are crowded. Every touch in a different place is another chance to land on a day they are paying attention.

Reason 02

Familiar beats cold

The name they saw giving a helpful answer on Monday is not a stranger in their inbox on Wednesday. Recognition is the start of trust.

Reason 03

People have a favourite channel

Some live in email. Some only answer LinkedIn messages. Some reply to a comment but never a cold email. Multi-channel meets each one where they answer.

Reason 04

Timing changes week to week

"Not now" in week one can become "good timing, actually" in week three. A campaign is still running when the moment arrives.

The same lead, two ways

Single channel

Email 1→Email 2→Email 3→Missed it on a busy week→Lead goes cold

Multi-channel

Helpful comment→Email 1→LinkedIn connect→Follow-up→Comment on their post→Last email→"Does Thursday work?"
  Email  Social
Four rules that keep it welcome, not annoying

Every touch adds something

A tip, an example, a question. Never "just bumping this".

Never the same message twice

Each channel gets its own short, native message.

Stop the moment they reply

A reply, even "not now", ends the sequence. Then talk.

Keep social human

Comments and DMs sent by you, in your own words.

Results vary with your market, your list and your message. Track your own numbers with the scorecard in chapter 09.

08 · Templates
Copy, fill the brackets, send

7 message templates for every touch.

Short beats clever. Every template opens on them, gives something useful and makes one small ask.

01 · Public reply · Play A, day 0
[Direct answer to their question in 1 or 2 lines]. What worked for us/our clients: [one specific tip]. If it helps, happy to share [the checklist / setup / example] I use.
Answer first. The offer at the end is optional and soft.
02 · DM · Play A, day 1
Hey [name], saw your post in [place] about [problem]. I put together [the thing you mentioned]. Want me to send it over?
Mentions the exact post, so it never feels cold. One yes or no question.
03 · LinkedIn connection note
Hi [name], enjoyed your [post/comment] on [topic]. I work on [your area] with [type of company] and would like to follow your updates.
No pitch. The goal is to be a familiar name, not to sell in 200 characters.
04 · First email · Play B, day 1
Subject: [their signal, in 3 to 5 words] Hi [name], saw [signal: you're hiring two account managers]. Usually that means [the problem it creates]. We help [similar companies] [result] without [the usual cost or pain]. Worth a 15 minute chat next week?
Opens on why now. One line on you. One clear, small ask.
05 · Follow-up · Play B, day 3
Hi [name], one example in case it's useful: [similar company] used [your approach] to [specific result]. Happy to show you how it would look for [their company].
Adds proof, not pressure. Never "just bumping this".
06 · Social touch · Play B, day 9
Hi [name], thanks for connecting. Curious how you're handling [problem] with the team growing. Anything you'd do differently?
A different angle from the emails. A question, not a repeat.
07 · Last email · Play B, day 7
Hi [name], I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If [problem] moves up the list later, just reply to this email and I'll pick it straight up. Good luck with [their signal].
Closing the loop politely often gets the reply the first two didn't, and leaves the door open.

Also note which touch got the reply. If most replies come after the second or third touch, that is your multi-channel campaign doing its job.

Email deliverability checklist

So your emails land in the inbox

  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set up on your sending domain
  • You send from a real mailbox, under a real name
  • New mailboxes start slowly and build volume over a few weeks
  • First emails are plain text, with no attachments and no images
  • One link at most, and none in the very first email if you can
  • Every email opens on something specific to that person
  • Bounced addresses are removed straight away
  • Anyone who asks to stop is never emailed again
  • You follow the email rules where your buyers are, such as GDPR in the UK and EU and CAN-SPAM in the US
Social safety checklist

So your accounts stay in good standing

  • You send replies and DMs yourself, from your own account
  • No browser bots or tools that click for you on LinkedIn
  • No copy and paste: each message is edited for the person
  • You read the rules of every group and subreddit first
  • Your profile says clearly who you are and what you do
  • Your public replies help even if the person never buys
  • You keep a sensible daily pace instead of bursts

This is why the social listening agent drafts and you send. Finding and writing are the slow parts; pressing send takes seconds and keeps your account yours.
